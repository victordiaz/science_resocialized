**Claim (hypothesis).** When people decide whether two briefly shown digits have the same numerical value, they answer "different" faster when the two values are far apart than when they are close, and this holds when the two digits are written in different notations. Predicted direction: mean RT for large-difference pairs is lower than for small-difference pairs.

**Design.** Participants see two digits and judge whether their numerical values are the same or different (the numerical task). Within participants: notation of the pair (3 levels: both Arabic digits, both Indian digits, mixed Arabic-Indian or Indian-Arabic) x numerical difference (2 levels: small = values 1 apart, pairs 12, 21, 89, 98; large = 6 or more apart, pairs 18, 19, 28, 29, 81, 82, 91, 92). Between participants: task order (numerical task first or second; the session also contains a second task in which participants judge whether the two digits look physically the same, analysed separately and not part of this claim).

**Procedure and stimuli.** Fixation cross 500 ms, then the digit pair for 200 ms. Participants press one key for "same value" and another for "different value", irrespective of notation. Experimental pairs use the digits 1, 2, 8, 9; same-value pairs (11, 22, 88, 99) and 72 filler trials using the digits 3 to 7 are included in the session but not analysed.

**Measure.** Response time (ms) on correct "different" responses to experimental pairs, averaged per participant within each notation x difference cell.

**Sample.** 32 adult native Arabic speakers who read both notations (university students, paid); one participant (ID 24) is dropped from all analyses by the replication's coding sheet, leaving 31 (16 and 15 in the two task orders).

**Exclusions.** Drop trials with RT < 200 ms or > 2,500 ms; drop filler trials (data column Distraction == 1); drop same-value pairs (Distance == 0); drop error trials (correct == 0); drop participant 24.

**Test.** 3 (notation, within) x 2 (numerical difference, within) x 2 (task order, between) mixed ANOVA on the cell-mean RTs, type III sums of squares. The term of interest is the **main effect of numerical difference**, F(1, 29), with its error mean square (MSE). Not the main effect of notation, not the difference x notation interaction, and not the analyses of the physical-sameness task.

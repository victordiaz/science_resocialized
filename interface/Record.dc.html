Hypothesis

Averaging two estimates of the same quantity given by one person, the second given in a later session, is more accurate than that person's first estimate alone. Direction: the mean squared error of the average of guess 1 and guess 2 is lower than the mean squared error of guess 1. The key test uses the delayed condition only.

Design

Within-subjects comparison in the delayed condition: participants answer eight percentage-estimation trivia questions online in session 1, and answer the same eight questions again, unannounced, in session 2 about three weeks later.

Sample

Undergraduate students who took part in both delayed sessions; 174 matched participants before exclusions, 140 after.

Test

Paired t-test of MSE(guess 1) vs MSE(average of the two guesses), df = n − 1. Effect size d_z = t / sqrt(n).

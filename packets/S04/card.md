**Claim (hypothesis).** People who merely watch a sequence of objects can pick up which features tend to follow which in time, for one feature dimension (colour) of multidimensional objects, separately from the objects as wholes. After watching a stream of coloured shapes in which colours follow each other in fixed triplets, people will judge colour-only triplets that occurred in the stream as more familiar than foil triplets that did not, at a rate above chance (50%).

**Design.** Single group; one-sample comparison against chance. Familiarisation phase: a continuous stream of single coloured nonsense shapes moving behind an occluder (about 1 s visible each), with a reduced correlation between shape and colour, built so that certain colour triplets recur in order. Test phase: on each trial the participant sees two triplet sequences of colour patches with no shape, one after the other, and chooses which one seems more familiar. 32 test trials per participant; 16 pit a real colour triplet from familiarisation against a foil ("real" trials), 16 pit a pseudo-triplet against a foil ("pseudo" trials).

**Measure.** For each participant, the proportion of the 16 "real" test trials on which the real triplet is chosen as more familiar (data field `correct` where `triplet_type == "real"`). Pseudo-triplet trials are discarded.

**Sample.** 15 adult volunteers, mostly undergraduate cognitive-science students, tested in German.

**Exclusions.** None (no participant dropped out; no data excluded). Participants who report noticing the triplets are kept in the key test.

**Test.** Two-sided one-sample t-test of the 15 per-participant proportions against 0.5; effect size d = t / sqrt(n).
